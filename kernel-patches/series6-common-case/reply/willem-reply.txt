Thanks Willem -- this is fair, and it pushed the design somewhere better.

You're right on both counts: +2500 LoC is too much for this, and "which
shapes deserve a fast path" is genuinely subjective -- "why GRE" is exactly
the right question and I don't have a principled answer for it. So rather
than defend the breadth, let me split it along the line your review draws.

In-kernel, keep only the one shape that isn't a judgment call: plain
eth + IPv4/IPv6 + TCP/UDP. It's the dominant shape on essentially every
machine and it's what skb_get_hash() sees most (RPS/RFS, fq/fq_codel/cake,
bonding). Reduced to that shape plus the BPF-lookup gating cleanup, the
series is:

    +377 / -50 across 5 files   (vs +2500 / -49 across 12)

i.e. patch 1 (gate the netns-BPF program lookup behind a static key) and
patch 2 (the eth+IP+TCP/UDP fast path) only. Everything subjective --
VLAN/QinQ, PPPoE, MPLS, IP-in-IP, GRE -- plus the per-shape counters and the
recursion bound they needed, all drop out.

For those subjective shapes I took your point that the BPF dissector already
offers a linear path -- and in fact the in-tree BPF flow dissector already
parses VLAN/QinQ/MPLS/IP-in-IP/GRE today; the only gap was PPPoE, which I
have a small separate patch for. [OPTIONAL, PENDING MEASUREMENT: on top of
that I have a loadable BPF flow dissector covering the same shapes with
fixed-offset extractors, byte-identical to the in-tree one over a per-shape
corpus, at [X ns/pkt vs Y for the in-tree BPF dissector]; it's [N lines].
Happy to post it separately for anyone who wants the broad coverage without
any in-kernel maintenance surface.]

On duplication/consistency: scoped to the single in-kernel shape the
divergence surface is one small extractor pair, default-off behind a static
key, reachable only by the two standard dissectors. It's byte-identical by
contract (miss -> slow path). If you'd want that guaranteed in-tree rather
than argued, I can include a small KUnit test (just this shape) that runs a
corpus through both paths and memcmp()s the flow_keys.

Would a v2 in that shape -- patch 1 + the eth+IP+TCP/UDP fast path, ~380
lines, optionally with the scoped equivalence test -- be worth a look?

Thanks again for the time.
